Boiler repair in Haverford, PA

A boiler heats water or makes steam and sends it to radiators, baseboard or in-floor loops. In Haverford, PA many older houses still heat with a boiler, and the plumber repairs leaks, failed pumps, faulty valves and ignition problems.

Boiler work is a heating job as much as a plumbing job. This page covers the plumbing side: leaks, pressure, circulators and the safety checks that come first.

When a boiler problem is urgent

A boiler leaking water, a pressure gauge in the red, a smell of gas or a house with no heat in winter all call for fast action. Gas smell is a leave-the-house problem. The others need a plumber soon.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility from outside. For no heat or a leak, switch the boiler off and call the line. Boilers are non-emergency for most repairs, but a leaking boiler can flood a basement quickly.

Boiler age and when to stop repairing

Boilers can last a long time when maintained, but parts wear out and replacements get harder to find. A repair on a boiler near the end of its life may only delay the larger cost.

Ask the plumber for both numbers, repair and replacement. If the boiler leaks from the body or the heat exchanger, it is generally time to replace. See water heater installation for the nearby water heating work.

Safe checks to do before you call

Simple steps that do not involve opening the boiler.

  1. Check the thermostatMake sure it is set to heat and the temperature is above the room.
  2. Check the pressure gaugeA normal cold reading is usually in a manufacturer-set range. Low or very high numbers should be reported to the plumber.
  3. Look for leaksCheck pipes, valves and the base of the boiler for water or rust.
  4. Check the power switch and breakerBoilers have their own switch, often on the wall near the unit.

What to tell the plumber when you call

Say whether the boiler runs at all, what the thermostat says and whether any radiators are warm. Mention any noises, leaks or smells and the age of the unit if you know it.

Photograph the pressure gauge and the label on the boiler. That information lets the plumber arrive with likely parts and cuts the time you spend in a cold house.

Where boiler work meets code

Relief valves, discharge piping and expansion tanks on a boiler are addressed by mechanical and plumbing codes. Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code draws on the International Code Council's model codes, including the International Plumbing Code, or IPC.

We say plainly when a job moves into heating work that needs a heating specialist. For the plumbing side, ask what permit applies and who pulls it.

Maintenance that prevents breakdowns

A yearly service visit catches worn parts before the first cold night. The plumber checks the water level, the pressure, the safety valve, the circulator and the venting.

Keep the area around the boiler clear, keep a carbon monoxide alarm nearby and bleed radiators each fall. If you have a leak that is not the boiler itself, see leak detection. For frozen pipes in the cold, see burst pipe repair.
