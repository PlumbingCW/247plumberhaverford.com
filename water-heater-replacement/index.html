Emergency water heater replacement in Haverford

A water heater that is leaking from the tank, rusting at the seams or no longer heating at all usually needs replacing, not repairing. In Haverford, PA the plumber arrives with a tank sized for your household, removes the old unit and installs the new one the same day when the part is in stock.

This page covers the replacement decision, the emergency steps for a failing tank and the cost factors. If you are comparing a tank with a tankless unit, our tankless water heater page covers that choice.

When a failing water heater is an emergency

A tank leaking from the body is the urgent case. Water pooling under the heater means the steel has failed, and a steady drip can turn into a flood while you are at work. Hot water that smells like rotten eggs is a nuisance. Water at the base of a tank is a deadline.

Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher what you see. The plumber can usually swap the unit the same day, and until then you should shut the water and power to the heater and keep the area clear.

Before the plumber arrives

These steps limit damage and speed up the visit.

Shut off the cold water

Close the valve on the cold inlet pipe above the heater. A quarter turn on a lever, or clockwise on a wheel.

Cut the power or gas

Switch off the breaker for an electric unit. For gas, turn the control to off. Do not relight a unit that smells of gas.

Clear the area

Move boxes, rugs and stored items away from the tank so the old unit can come out.

Check the label

Photograph the data plate on the tank. It shows capacity, fuel type and age, which helps the plumber bring the right replacement.

Repair or replace: how to decide

A repair makes sense when the tank is sound. This table shows the signs that point each way.

What you seeUsually meansLikely answer
Water at the base of the tankFailed tank or a leaking fittingReplace if the leak is from the tank body
No hot water, tank coldBurned-out element, thermostat or pilot problemRepair if the tank is young and dry
Rust-colored hot waterCorroding tank interiorReplace, especially on an older unit
Rumbling or poppingSediment on the tank bottomFlush or replace depending on age and condition

Tanks commonly run about 8 to 12 years, and warranties usually track that range. If yours is near the end and needs a costly repair, replacement is usually the better spend. For smaller issues, see water heater repair.

Choosing the size and type

The right tank depends on how many people use hot water at the same time. A bathroom group of showers and a dishwasher cycle together draw more than a single sink. A plumber will ask about your household and your peak mornings before recommending a capacity.

You also choose the fuel. Many Haverford houses already have a gas line to the heater, and switching to electric means an electrical upgrade. Staying with the same fuel keeps the job simple, while a change of fuel adds work and permits.

Plumbing work in Haverford Township is done by contractors licensed with the Township, and the Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 handles permit questions. The Township says plumbing work is inspected for compliance with the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code.

Ask your plumber whether your replacement needs a permit and who pulls it. In neighboring Ardmore and Wynnewood, parts of town fall under Lower Merion Township, which has its own office. Our local pages, such as plumbing in Ardmore, note which office to call.

What a replacement visit looks like

A typical swap takes a few hours when the new tank is on the truck.

  1. Confirm the setupThe plumber checks fuel, venting, shutoff valves, the expansion tank and the drain.
  2. Drain and removeThe old tank is drained, disconnected and carried out. Disposal is part of the job.
  3. Install and connectThe new tank is set, connected with new flex lines and valves, and vented or wired to code.
  4. Test and explainThe unit is filled, fired and checked for leaks, and you hear how to set the temperature and where the shutoff is.

Water heater replacement cost factors

The final number depends on several choices, and the price is stated before work starts.

What moves the price

Tank size and typeA larger tank or a higher-efficiency model costs more than a standard one.
Fuel and ventingA gas unit needs a safe vent, and a change of fuel adds parts and labor.
Code updatesOlder installs may need a new expansion tank, valves, pan or seismic and thermal protections the current code expects.
AccessA tight closet or a basement staircase adds time to carry the old unit out.

A sudden tank failure may be covered by homeowners insurance for the water damage, but the tank itself usually is not. Call (610) 672-7601 for a written price for your tank before work begins.

What not to do when a tank fails

Do not relight a gas unit that is leaking or that smells of gas. Do not keep running an electric unit with a leaking tank, since water near live parts is dangerous. Do not cap or plug the relief valve to stop a drip.

Shut the cold supply and the power or gas, then call. Leave the tank in place for the plumber to remove.

Code points behind a tank swap

A like-for-like swap still sits inside the code. The International Plumbing Code, or IPC, on which Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code is built, speaks to relief valves, discharge pipes and thermal expansion for water heaters.

Ask the plumber to show you each of those parts on your new tank before the job ends. Haverford Township's Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 can say whether your swap needs a filing.

Safety settings worth knowing

Most manufacturers and safety groups suggest setting the thermostat near 120 degrees Fahrenheit to lower scald risk. A temperature and pressure relief valve must be present, discharge to a safe place and never be capped.

If you hear your valve dripping, call. It may be reacting to high pressure or heat, and it should not be plugged to stop the drip. See also water heater installation for new-build and relocation work.
