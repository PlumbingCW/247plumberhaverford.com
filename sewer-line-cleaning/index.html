Emergency sewer line cleaning for main-line backups

Sewer line cleaning clears the main drain that carries every fixture's wastewater out of the house and into the lateral, and it is what the plumber does when sewage shows up in a tub, a shower or a basement floor drain. In Haverford, PA the job is one of the most common night calls we dispatch.

The main line is bigger than a branch drain, so the tools are bigger too. A heavy cable machine with cutter heads, a camera and, when needed, a hydro jet restore flow and show what stopped it.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewer backup is a health problem as well as a plumbing one, because the water is contaminated. Keep children and pets away, avoid touching the water and call the 24/7 line before you start cleaning.

Do not run water anywhere in the house. If you can reach the main cleanout safely, tell the dispatcher where it is. The plumber clears the line, checks it on camera and tells you what caused the stoppage.

While you wait

Keep the damage contained until the line is open.

Stop all water use

No toilets, sinks, showers or laundry. A stopped main sends every drop to your lowest drain.

Keep electricity away

Do not step into standing water near outlets or appliances. Use the breaker only if the panel is dry and reachable.

Document the loss

Photograph affected rooms and belongings before cleanup. Insurance questions come later and photos help.

Ventilate

Open a window if you can do so safely, and keep people out of the affected room until the line is cleared.

Why main lines stop

Main lines usually stop for four reasons. Tree roots enter at a joint and form a mat. Grease and flushed wipes build into a plug. A section of pipe settles and holds water, which collects solids. Or the pipe cracks and soil fills it.

Cleaning treats the first two, and repair treats the last two. The camera tells which one you have, so you do not pay for cleaning that cannot last. The sewer line repair page explains the next step when the pipe itself is damaged.

Which signs point to the main line

Several clues separate a main-line problem from a single clogged fixture.

Gurgling in more than one drain

Air pushed back through traps by a blockage downstream makes the sounds.

Water in the lowest drain

A basement floor drain or shower pan is the first place a stopped main shows itself.

A toilet that bubbles when the washer drains

Water has nowhere to go but back to the nearest open drain.

Sewage smell around the foundation

A cracked lateral or an open cleanout can release the smell outside.

The Haverford Township lateral rule

The Haverford Township Code states that sewer service laterals are the property of the customer, and the Township says it is generally not liable for sewer, drain or sump pump damage. In practice that means the stoppage between your house and the main in the street is yours to clear.

The Township's notice also warns that standard homeowners policies do not cover sewer backups or flooding, and that coverage is available as an endorsement. It is worth a call to your agent before the next storm season.

If you are in Ardmore, Wynnewood or Bryn Mawr, parts of those towns fall under Lower Merion Township instead, which has its own rules. Our local guides, such as plumbing in Wynnewood, say which office to call.

Why a cleanout makes the job faster

Model plumbing codes, including the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, call for cleanouts on building sewers. A cleanout at the house lets the plumber send a cable or camera straight into the line.

If your cleanout is buried or missing, ask the plumber to expose or add one. It pays for itself the next time the line backs up.

After the line is open

A clear main is not the end of the story if it stopped once. Ask to see the camera footage, ask whether roots or a low spot are present and ask what cleaning interval, if any, is reasonable for your line.

Many Haverford homeowners also install a backwater valve, a device that blocks flow from the street side. It is a plumbing installation job, covered under plumbing installation, and worth discussing if your basement has a history of backups. For a line that clogs often, see hydro jetting.
