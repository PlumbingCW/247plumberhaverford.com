Plumbing problems we see most in Ardmore

Ardmore is a busy Main Line commercial hub with apartments, shops and restaurants packed close together, and older houses a few blocks off the main avenue. That mix produces two kinds of calls: backed-up drains in older buildings and failed water heaters in homes where the tank has been working since before the current owners arrived.

Part of Ardmore sits inside Haverford Township and part sits in Lower Merion Township, so the municipality that issues permits depends on your street. Tell the dispatcher your address and the plumber will tell you which building office to call before work that needs a permit.

Restaurant and shop drains

Grease and heavy use clog the lines under Ardmore's commercial strip. A cable or jet clears the line, and a schedule keeps it clear. See commercial plumbing.

Older house main lines

Houses built decades ago often have clay or cast iron laterals that roots find. The cure starts with a camera, covered in sewer inspection.

Water heaters at the end of life

A tank leaking in a basement is the classic Ardmore morning call. Same-day swaps are the norm. See water heater replacement.

Apartment leaks

A leak that starts in one unit and shows up in another needs fast shutoff and a plumber who can find the source. See leak detection.

Before we arrive in Ardmore

If you have a burst pipe or an active leak, work through these four steps while help is on the way.

Close the main valve

Find the shutoff where the water line enters your building or unit and turn it fully off.

Cut power near the water

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Clear the area

Move electronics, papers and rugs out of the wet zone before the plumber arrives.

Two townships, one phone number

The Haverford Township Building and Codes Department can be reached at 610-446-1000 for addresses inside the Township. Addresses in the Lower Merion part of Ardmore go through Lower Merion's own building office. The call to us is the same either way.

Haverford Township also states that sewer laterals belong to the customer, so a main-line problem on a Haverford Township address is the owner's to repair. Ask the plumber to note which side of the line you are on before a repair is scoped.
