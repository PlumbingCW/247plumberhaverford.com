Emergency sewer line repair in Haverford

Sewer line repair fixes a broken, cracked, offset or root-damaged pipe between your house and the municipal main. In Haverford, PA the repair is usually decided after a camera inspection, and it ranges from a spot repair or lining to a section replacement.

A cleaned line that clogs again within weeks, a sewer smell in the yard or a soft patch of lawn over the pipe all suggest damage. When the pipe is cracked or collapsed, cleaning only delays the real repair.

When a sewer problem is an emergency

A line that has collapsed or separated can stop completely, and that is a sewage backup. If the camera shows a break in a line that has stopped, repair is urgent because the house cannot drain.

Call the 24/7 line. The plumber clears what can be cleared, runs a camera, explains the damage and tells you which repair is available. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and a price is stated before work starts.

Symptoms of a damaged sewer line

Callers describe damage in simple terms, and each one has a likely cause.

Repeated clogs in the same line

Roots or an offset joint snag paper and grease. The line clears and clogs again.

Soggy ground or a sinkhole in the yard

A leak in the lateral can wash soil away, and the dip shows where the pipe failed.

Sewage smell outside

A crack releases gas along the pipe's path.

Slow drains in many fixtures

A partly collapsed pipe restricts flow even when it is not fully stopped.

Ask the plumber to show you the footage and mark where the damage sits. A repair offered without footage is a guess. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what a good inspection looks like.

Pipe lining, in plain terms

Pipe lining inserts a resin-soaked tube into the old pipe and cures it into a new inner pipe, which seals cracks and blocks roots without opening the yard. It works on pipes that are still round and on runs with a few broken joints.

It is not magic. A lining reduces the pipe's diameter slightly, it needs a clear path for the liner and it depends on the host pipe not being collapsed. The plumber should confirm all of that on camera before recommending it.

Digging, 811 and Township rules

Any excavation near your lateral should begin with a call to 811 so that buried utilities are marked before the dig. Skipping that call is a safety risk and, in many places, a legal one.

In Haverford Township, plumbing work is performed by contractors licensed with the Township, and the Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 handles permit questions. The Township states that work is inspected for compliance with the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code. Ask your plumber to explain which permits apply to your lateral repair.

Remember that the lateral is the customer's property under the Township Code. The repair is your responsibility, so a clear scope in writing matters.

What to do before a sewer repair visit

Stop using water in the house if the line is backing up. Keep children and pets away from standing sewage and avoid touching it with bare hands. If you plan to dig, call 811 first so utilities can be marked.

Locate your cleanout and clear the path to it. Write down when the problem started and which fixtures were affected, since that helps the plumber find the damaged section.

Standards that apply to sewer pipe repairs

Sewer pipe material, slope and joints are governed by the plumbing code in force locally, which for Pennsylvania is based on the International Code Council's International Plumbing Code, or IPC. A repair that changes the pipe usually needs inspection.

Pipe liners and replacement pipe also carry their own product standards. Ask the plumber which standard the product meets and get the warranty in writing.

Cleaning first, then repair

The right order is usually clean, inspect, then repair. Cleaning gets the house draining again, the camera finds the damage, and a repair is chosen once the facts are known. That keeps you from paying for a trench when a lining would have worked.

If your line only needs cleaning, see sewer line cleaning. If it is slow because of grease and scale, hydro jetting may be enough. In Bryn Mawr and Ardmore, older lines are common, as our guide to plumbing in Bryn Mawr explains.
