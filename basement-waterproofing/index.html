Basement waterproofing in Haverford, PA

Basement waterproofing keeps groundwater, seepage and condensation out of the lowest level of your house. In Haverford, PA the plumber looks at the source first, then recommends drainage, a sump system, crack repair or exterior fixes that match what is actually happening.

This page covers the common causes of a wet basement, the options that work and the quick fixes that do not. If your basement is flooding right now, call the 24/7 line and see sump pump repair.

Find the water source before paying for a fix

A wet basement has several possible causes, and the wrong fix wastes money. Water can come through floor cracks, wall seams, the floor-to-wall joint, window wells, a failed pipe or a drain line that backs up.

A simple test is to tape a square of plastic wrap to a dry wall or floor for two days. Moisture on the outside of the plastic is condensation from the air. Moisture underneath is coming through the masonry.

Interior drain systems and sumps

An interior drain system collects water at the floor-wall joint and sends it to a sump pit, where a pump moves it out. It is a common fix for seepage from rising groundwater, and it can be done without digging up the yard.

The pump is the critical part. A system with a weak pump or no backup can fail the first time the power goes out. Read the sump pump repair page for pump types and backups.

Exterior fixes that cost little

Many wet basements are helped by cheaper steps outside. Gutters that overflow, downspouts that empty by the foundation and soil that slopes toward the house all send water to the wall.

Extend downspouts several feet away, clean gutters and check that the soil slopes away from the foundation. These steps do not fix every problem, but they remove a common cause for little cost.

What not to buy first

Some products promise more than they deliver.

Waterproof paint alone

It can hide dampness for a short time but does not stop water under pressure.

Crack sealant with no drainage plan

A sealed crack can open again if the cause is groundwater pressure.

A dehumidifier for a leak

It helps with humidity but cannot keep up with a steady seep.

A quote with no inspection

A price given over the phone cannot account for the cause.

Codes that touch drainage and sumps

Sump pits, discharge pipes and backwater valves fall under the plumbing code, including the International Plumbing Code, or IPC, as adopted locally. Pumps and outlets near them fall under the National Electrical Code, or NEC.

A permit may be required for a new system. Ask the Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 before work starts.

Humidity, mold and what a plumber does and does not do

A damp basement can grow mold on walls, boxes and stored items. A plumber fixes water entry and the pumps and pipes that handle it. Mold remediation is a specialist trade, and we say when to call one.

Cut down on moisture by running a dehumidifier, venting dryers outdoors and keeping stored items off the floor. If you smell mold or see growth over a large area, bring in a specialist.
