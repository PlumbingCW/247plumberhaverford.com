Plumbing installation in Haverford, PA

Plumbing installation adds or moves fixtures, lines and equipment: a new bathroom, a kitchen remodel, a laundry, a utility sink, a backwater valve or a gas line for a range. In Haverford, PA the plumber plans the work, pulls any needed permit, installs the fixtures and tests the system before closing walls.

This page covers what installation involves, what to prepare and how to get a clear quote.

Common installation jobs

Most calls fall into a handful of categories. These are new or replaced toilets, sinks and tubs, a shower valve and a dishwasher or disposal hookup. Others are a washer box, an outdoor spigot, a basement utility sink and a backwater valve to protect the sewer line.

A plumber can also add a laundry line, a basement bathroom rough-in and a gas stub for an appliance. Each has its own permit and inspection steps, so ask what applies.

Jobs and what they involve

Scope varies, so the walk-through matters.

How an installation job runs

The order protects your walls and your inspection.

  1. Plan and quoteThe plumber measures, checks existing lines and writes a scope with a price.
  2. PermitIf the work needs one, the permit is pulled before it starts.
  3. Rough-inPipes and drains are run and tested before walls close. Many inspections happen at this stage.
  4. Finish and testFixtures are set, the system is tested and you get a walkthrough.

Permits and inspections

Haverford Township says plumbing work is done by Township-licensed contractors and is inspected for compliance with the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code. The Building and Codes Department can be reached at 610-446-1000.

Parts of Ardmore, Wynnewood and Bryn Mawr fall under Lower Merion Township and parts of Broomall and Drexel Hill fall under other townships, each with its own office. Your plumber should know which one applies to your address and who pulls the permit.

The code behind fixture, drain and vent decisions

Fixture spacing, drain sizing and venting come from the plumbing code adopted in Pennsylvania, which is based on the International Code Council's International Plumbing Code, or IPC. The township enforces the edition that applies to your permit.

That is why the quote should name the drain size and vent plan. If a bid is silent on those items, ask what code the plumber is building to.

What to have ready

Bring fixtures you want installed, or at least the model numbers, before the plumber starts. Know where the main shutoff is and clear access to the work area.

Ask for the quote in writing, including permit, inspection and cleanup. If your project also needs a new water heater, see water heater installation. For old supply lines in the walls you plan to open, see repiping.
