Sink garbage disposal repair: what you can fix and what you cannot

Most garbage disposal problems come down to a jam, a tripped reset, a leaking seal or a motor that has given out. A jam and a reset are quick fixes you can do in minutes. A leak or a dead motor usually means a plumber visit or a new unit.

This guide covers the safe checks first, then the repairs that belong to a plumber in Haverford, PA.

Safety comes before every step

Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is off. Turn the switch off, unplug the unit or switch off its breaker and use tongs or pliers to remove anything you can see.

A disposal blade can start if the switch is bumped, and the injury is severe. If you cannot confirm the power is off, call a plumber.

Clearing a jam

Most humming disposals have a jam.

  1. Power offSwitch off the unit and unplug it or switch off the breaker.
  2. Use the hex keyInsert the wrench into the socket on the bottom and turn it back and forth until the blades move freely.
  3. Remove debrisUse tongs to pull out any object you can see.
  4. Press resetPress the red reset button on the bottom of the unit, then restore power and test with water running.

Haverford Township and the kitchen drain behind your disposal

Haverford Township says plumbing work done by contractors is performed by Township-licensed plumbers and inspected under the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code. A disposal swap is usually simple, but the Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 can confirm whether a new electrical connection needs any filing.

Haverford Township Code treats the sewer lateral as the customer's property. A disposal that backs up into a double sink may point to a blocked kitchen line or lateral, which is the owner's to clear.

Repair or replace

A seal replacement or a jam clearing makes sense on a newer unit. A burned-out motor or a cracked housing on an older unit is better replaced. A new standard unit installs in a short visit, and the plumber checks the dishwasher connection.

See garbage disposal repair for how the visit runs. For clogs behind the disposal, see drain cleaning.

What should never go in

Avoid grease, fibrous vegetables like celery, large amounts of coffee grounds, rice and pasta, bones and eggshells in bulk. Run cold water while it works and for a few seconds afterward.

Those habits extend the life of the unit and keep the line behind it clear. We serve Drexel Hill and Wynnewood as well as Haverford.

Reading the sound the disposal makes

A hum means the motor has power and cannot spin. A click or nothing at all points to a power or reset problem. A loud grinding of metal usually means something hard fell into the chamber, such as a spoon or a bottle cap.

Describe the sound to the plumber if you call. It tells them whether to bring a jam tool, a reset kit or a replacement unit.

Dishwasher connection and the knockout plug

When a new disposal is installed on a sink with a dishwasher, the plug in the dishwasher inlet must be knocked out. If it is not, the dishwasher cannot drain, and water backs up into the machine.

A plumber handles this as a matter of course. If you install the unit yourself and the dishwasher stops draining, check this first.

Cost, noise and choosing a replacement

Disposals vary in motor size, sound insulation and grinding stages. A larger motor handles more and often runs quieter. A basic unit is fine for a small household that cooks simply.

Pick a unit that matches your habits. If you cook often with a lot of scraps, a stronger motor is worth the extra. If you rarely use the disposal, a basic model will do.

Cleaning and odors without chemicals

A smelly disposal is usually a layer of food residue on the walls and under the splash guard. Run cold water, grind a handful of ice cubes and a few citrus peels, and scrub the splash guard with a brush and dish soap.

Skip bleach and strong cleaners, which can damage seals and rubber parts. If the smell remains after cleaning, check the drain line for a partial blockage.

What a replacement visit includes

The plumber turns off power, disconnects the old unit and the drain, mounts the new unit, reconnects the drain and the dishwasher hose and tests for leaks. The old unit is hauled away.

If the sink flange is in good shape, it can often be reused. If it leaks, it is replaced with the new unit, which is why a leak at the top of a disposal often becomes a full replacement.

What to do if the disposal leaks right now

Turn the switch off and unplug the unit or switch off its breaker. Close the shutoff valves under the sink if water is running, and set a bucket under the leak.

Do not reach into the disposal and do not run it again until the leak is traced. Dry the cabinet floor and call a plumber if the housing is cracked.

When the smell or slowdown is in the line

A disposal that spins well while the sink drains slowly is telling you the line below is the problem. A cable or jet is the fix, not a new disposal.

If the line backs up every few weeks, a camera inspection shows why. A plumber can bring one along on the visit.
