Emergencies

The plumbing emergencies we answer around the clock

Most emergency plumber calls in Haverford fall into six groups. Each one has a page that explains the cause, the repair options and what to do before the plumber arrives, so you can read up while you wait on hold or in the car.

Clogged and backed-up drains

A kitchen line that gurgles or a basement floor drain that rises needs a cable or a jet fast, and our crews handle both under emergency drain cleaning and hydro jetting.

Burst and leaking pipes

When water is coming through a ceiling or a wall, emergency burst pipe repair stops the flow first, and leak detection finds the hidden source if you cannot see it.

No hot water or a leaking tank

A tank that has quit or started to leak is a water heater repair call, and a unit past saving becomes water heater replacement, often the same day.

Sewer backups

Sewage in a tub or floor drain points to the main line, so we send a camera and a cutter through sewer line repair and sewer line cleaning.

Wet basements and failed sump pumps

When a sump pump stops during a storm, water can reach finished space quickly, and basement waterproofing keeps it from returning.

Toilets, faucets and disposals

An overflowing toilet, a faucet that will not shut off or a leaking disposal all have a fix on the same visit, from toilet repair to garbage disposal repair.

Planned work gets the same licensed crews. If you are comparing a tank against a tankless unit, start with water heater installation or the guide to tankless water heaters, and for aging supply pipes see repiping. The full list sits on the plumbing services page.

Local knowledge

Haverford homes and what they ask of a plumber

Haverford Township is a Delaware County community of neighborhoods that grew up in different decades, and the plumbing in each one ages differently. Havertown, Brookline, Beechwood, Oakmont, Manoa, Llanerch, Chatham Park and Bon Air each began as separate developments, so a house on one side of Darby Road can hold a very different pipe system from one a mile away.

That is why the first thing a plumber does is look, not guess. A house with decades-old drain lines may need a camera before a cable, while a post-war development like Chatham Park may simply have a failing water heater. Telling the dispatcher your street and the age of the house helps the plumber bring the right parts.

Older landmarks tell the same story. The Federal School, a schoolhouse from 1797 on the National Register of Historic Places, shows how long buildings in this township have stood, and long-standing buildings are exactly where galvanized supply lines and clay or cast iron drains turn up behind the walls. The Grange Estate in Havertown, a mansion the Township has owned since 1974, is a reminder that big old houses here carry a lot of pipe to maintain.

Commercial corridors matter too. Haverford Township's long-range planning document describes buildings along Eagle Road as dating from around the 1950s, which means small shops and offices with aging restrooms, floor drains and grease lines. Our commercial plumbing page explains how after-hours calls for businesses work.

Water, creeks and the basement

The township sits along Darby Creek and Cobbs Creek, and its planning document notes that storms have been getting more frequent and more severe, with new construction already barred from the 100-year floodplain. For a homeowner the practical meaning is simple: if your basement has ever taken on water, treat the sump pump as safety equipment and test it before the next storm.

Sewer backups deserve the same attention. Under the Township Code, a sewer service lateral is the property of the customer, and the Township says it is generally not liable for sewer or drain damage. If sewage comes up a floor drain, the repair on your side of the connection is your responsibility, which is why quick sewer line cleaning and a camera look matter.

Licensing

Check the license before you let anyone in

Haverford Township states that contractors performing work in the Township must be licensed with the Township, and its plumbing permit process asks for a current Township contractor license, a Pennsylvania home improvement contractor registration and a certificate of insurance. Lower Merion Township, which covers Ardmore, Wynnewood and much of Bryn Mawr, requires plumbing masters to be licensed there as well.

Pennsylvania's Attorney General requires contractors who do at least $5,000 of home improvement work a year to register, and you can reach the consumer line at 1-888-520-6680 to check a registration. Our plumbers are licensed and insured, and you should ask any plumber you call, including us, for the numbers and then verify them.
