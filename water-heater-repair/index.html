Emergency water heater repair in Haverford

Water heater repair covers the parts that fail before the tank does: heating elements, thermostats, igniters, thermocouples, valves and anode rods. In Haverford, PA a plumber can often fix a no-hot-water problem in a single visit if the tank is still sound.

A repair is worth it when the tank is young and dry. If the tank itself leaks, the page on water heater replacement is the better fit.

No hot water: when to call right away

A cold shower is an inconvenience, but a few symptoms are safety problems. A gas smell, a tripped breaker that keeps tripping, scorching water or a pressure valve that sprays all call for an immediate call.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility before anything else. For other problems, the 24/7 line is open and the dispatcher will tell you what to turn off while the plumber is on the way.

Symptoms and likely causes

Different symptoms point to different parts. A plumber confirms by testing, and these are the usual suspects.

No hot water at all, electric

A failed heating element, a tripped high-limit switch or a bad thermostat. All are repairable parts.

No hot water at all, gas

A pilot that will not stay lit, a failed thermocouple or a faulty gas valve, often a quick fix.

Water is lukewarm

One of two elements has failed, or the dip tube has cracked and mixes cold water in.

Water runs out fast

Sediment is taking up tank volume, or one element has quit, so the tank refills slowly.

The age question

Age decides whether a repair is smart. A tank under eight years old with a failed element is an easy repair. A tank past twelve years with a bad gas valve is often a poor use of money, because the next failure may be the tank.

Check the serial number on the data plate. Many manufacturers encode the date in it, and the plumber can read it for you. If the unit is in the gray zone, ask for a price on both repair and replacement and choose with the numbers in front of you.

Sediment, noise and the flush

A rumbling tank is usually one with sediment baked onto the bottom. The noise is trapped steam bubbling through grit. Flushing the tank can reduce it, but heavily scaled tanks sometimes do not respond.

Flushing is a maintenance task that many homeowners skip, and some tanks respond to it best when it starts early. Ask the plumber what your tank looks like when it is drained, and whether a yearly flush makes sense for you.

Recurring problems that point to something else

If a heater trips its breaker repeatedly or the relief valve drips every day, the cause may be in the house, not the heater. High water pressure, a failed expansion tank or a bad shutoff all produce the same symptoms.

Pressure problems tie into your whole plumbing system, and in some cases a new water heater will not fix them. For a leak that starts at the connections, see leak detection, and for a new tank, water heater replacement.
