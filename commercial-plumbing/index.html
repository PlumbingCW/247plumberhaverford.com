Emergency commercial plumbing in Haverford

Commercial plumbing covers restaurants, shops, offices, medical suites, apartment buildings and other business properties where a plumbing failure stops work. In Haverford, PA the plumber handles backups, leaks, grease lines, water heaters, backflow devices and fixture repairs for small and mid-size buildings.

This page covers what a business needs to know about response, grease and permits.

When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

A restaurant with a backed-up kitchen line, an office with an overflowing restroom or a retail space with water running through the ceiling cannot stay open. The cost of downtime often exceeds the repair itself.

Call the 24/7 line and give the address, what is happening and whether the water can be shut off. The plumber arrives with the right equipment, clears or stops the problem and tells you what comes next.

Common commercial calls

Businesses tend to call about a handful of problems.

Kitchen and grease lines

Grease builds in drains and traps, and a stoppage shuts the kitchen. Regular cleaning prevents it.

Restroom backups

A blocked main or a clogged fixture takes the restroom out of service.

Water heaters and boilers

Failures close restaurants and salons. Commercial units have their own sizing.

Leaks above ceilings

A split pipe or a failed fitting can damage stock, equipment and finishes.

Grease traps, interceptors and keeping the line open

Restaurants and food service businesses often need a grease trap or interceptor to catch fats before they enter the sewer. These devices must be cleaned on a schedule, or grease passes through and builds up downstream.

A hydro jet is often the best tool for a greasy line, and it is the reason regular jetting is part of many restaurant maintenance plans. See hydro jetting for how it works. For a stopped line, see drain cleaning.

Rules and permits

Haverford Township says plumbing work is performed by contractors licensed with the Township and inspected under the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code. Commercial jobs may also involve health, fire and zoning rules, depending on the use.

Backflow prevention, grease interceptors and food service plumbing can have additional rules. Ask the Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 what applies to your property, and have your plumber document test and repair work for your records.

What to have ready when you call for a business

Give the address, the name of the person on site and the nature of the problem. Say whether the water can be shut off and where the shutoff is. If you are a tenant, tell the dispatcher who the landlord or manager is.

That information saves time at the door. Keep a list of shutoffs and cleanouts with your emergency numbers so any shift manager can find them.

What to do before the plumber arrives at your business

Shut off the water to the affected area if you can reach the valve, and keep customers and staff away from wet floors. Cover or move stock and equipment that water could reach, and do not use electrical equipment standing in water.

Photograph the damage before cleanup and write down the time the problem started. Those notes help with any insurance claim and help the plumber understand what happened.

Codes that shape commercial plumbing

Commercial plumbing carries added requirements for fixture counts, grease handling and backflow protection. These sit within the Uniform Construction Code in Pennsylvania, which draws on the International Code Council's International Plumbing Code, or IPC.

Health and fire rules may apply as well. Ask the Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 and the plumber what applies to your use.

Prevention for businesses

A preventive plan costs less than an emergency call. Schedule grease line cleaning, test backflow devices, check water heater safety valves and know where every shutoff is.

Train staff on what not to flush or pour down drains, and post the plumber's number near the manager's desk. If you manage a multi-unit building, our sewer line cleaning page covers main-line backups, and water heater replacement covers the tank side.
