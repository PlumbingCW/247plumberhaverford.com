Tankless Water Heater Installation: Haverford, PA Guide

A tankless install is more than a swap. Here is what gets checked, what gets built and what changes the price.

What tankless water heater installation involves in a Haverford home

A tankless water heater installation replaces a hot water tank with a wall-mounted unit that heats water on demand. The work is bigger than a tank swap because the unit needs a stronger gas line or a heavier electrical circuit, a sealed vent and sometimes a condensate drain.

This guide walks through the steps, the checks that come first and the choices that change the price in Haverford, PA.

Before any work: the checks

A good installer checks four things before quoting. The first is gas: line size, meter capacity and the total gas load of the house. The second is electrical: panel space and the circuit the unit needs. The third is venting: a path to the outside that meets the manufacturer's rules. The fourth is the water side, including pressure and hardness.

These checks decide whether tankless works in your house and what it will cost. A quote given without them is a guess.

The installation sequence

A typical gas tankless install runs in this order.

  1. Plan and permitThe plumber confirms the model, the location, the vent path and whether a permit and inspection apply.
  2. Remove the tankThe old heater is drained, disconnected and carried out.
  3. Mount the unit and run the ventThe new unit is fixed to the wall and the vent is run through the wall or roof.
  4. Run gas, water and powerA larger gas line, water connections with service valves and electrical power are connected.
  5. Set up the condensate drainHigh-efficiency units produce acidic condensate that must drain safely.
  6. Test and commissionThe plumber fires the unit, checks combustion and leaks, sets the temperature and walks you through it.

What Haverford Township says about plumbing permits

Haverford Township says plumbing work done by contractors is performed by Township-licensed plumbers and is inspected for compliance with the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code. The Building and Codes Department at 610-446-1000 can say what applies to a tankless install.

We do not quote your water hardness from memory. It varies by street and by supplier, so ask your water supplier for its report or test your tap. A hardness figure from a real test tells you how often to descale.

Sizing: the part people get wrong

Tankless units are rated by how many gallons per minute they can heat to a set rise in temperature. Size by adding the fixtures you may run at once. Two showers and a dishwasher together are a bigger demand than one shower.

A unit that is too small gives a lukewarm shower when two fixtures run. A unit that is too large costs more than needed. A plumber who asks about your peak mornings is doing it right. For the comparison with a tank, see tankless water heaters.

Choosing between gas and electric tankless

Gas units deliver higher flow and suit larger households, but they need gas, a vent and a condensate drain. Electric units are simpler to vent and often smaller, but they draw a heavy electrical load and are best for smaller demand or point-of-use applications.

Your existing fuel and your panel capacity usually decide the answer. A plumber and an electrician can check both before you commit to a model.

Recirculation and the wait for hot water

A tankless heater makes hot water fast, but the water still has to travel through the pipe to the faucet. A long run means a wait and wasted water. A recirculation line or a small buffer tank keeps hot water near the fixtures.

If waiting for hot water bothers you now, ask about it at the walk-through. A tankless unit alone does not fix a long pipe run.

Maintenance you will do each year

Plan on a yearly check of the vent, the intake and the water filter screen. Descale the heat exchanger on the schedule the maker gives, more often if your water is hard.

A service valve kit installed with the unit makes descaling a short job. Without it, flushing is harder and often skipped, which is how warranties get voided.

Rebates, warranties and fine print

Some manufacturers and utilities offer rebates for high-efficiency units, and some require installation by a licensed contractor to keep the warranty. Read the terms before you buy, and keep the invoice and the model number.

Registration deadlines are short. Registering the unit within the window can double the warranty period in some cases, so do it the week the unit goes in.

Manufacturer rules and code rules for tankless

A tankless install must follow the manufacturer's instructions and the plumbing and fuel gas codes that apply locally, including the International Plumbing Code, or IPC. Vent materials, clearances and condensate disposal are common inspection items.

Keep the installation manual with the unit. An inspector or a future owner will want to see that the install matches it.

After the install

Keep the manual and the model number, and mark the service date. Descale on the manufacturer's schedule and keep the vent clear of leaves and snow. A carbon monoxide alarm near the unit is a good habit.
