Large older homes and what they do to plumbing

Bryn Mawr is known for large older houses, stone and brick construction and mature trees. That is a good setting for living and a demanding one for plumbing. Big houses have long pipe runs, several bathrooms and often more than one water heater, so a single failure can touch many fixtures.

Houses of that age often still carry original drain lines, and tree roots are a common reason a main line fails. Ask for a camera look before agreeing to any repair on an old line, and keep the footage for your records.

Housing era and the plumbing to expect

The era of the house points to the likely materials and the likely failures.

Era of the housePlumbing to expectWhat tends to fail
Early twentieth centuryCast iron or clay drains, galvanized supplyRoots, scale and low pressure
Mid centuryCopper supply, cast iron drainsPinholes and joint failures
Later additionsMixed copper and plasticFittings and old-to-new connections
Recent renovationsPEX or copper with new fixturesInstallation errors, not age

These are tendencies, not rules. A camera or a pressure test tells you what your house has. If the supply pipes are failing, our repiping page explains the options.

Before we arrive in Bryn Mawr

If a pipe has burst or a leak is active in a large house, start with these steps.

Shut the main

Protect what matters

Move rugs, art and electronics out of the wet area right away. Larger houses have more to lose.

Cut power safely

Protecting finishes during a repair

In a large older house, plaster, hardwood and built-in cabinetry are valuable. Tell the plumber what you want protected and ask how they plan to cover floors and furniture. A good crew lays drop cloths and shoe covers before they start.

If a wall must be opened, ask who will patch it. Many plumbers leave finish work to a painter or carpenter, and it helps to know before the wall is cut.
