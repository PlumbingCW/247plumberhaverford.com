Drain cleaning in Haverford, and when it cannot wait

Drain cleaning removes whatever is blocking a pipe, whether that is grease in a kitchen line, hair in a tub trap or roots in the line under your yard. In Haverford, PA the job usually starts with a cable, moves to a camera if the clog keeps returning and ends with a jet if the pipe wall is coated.

This page covers kitchen sinks, showers, floor drains and the main line, plus the emergency steps for a drain that is already backing up. If sewage is rising in the lowest drain of the house, jump to the section on emergency sewer backups below and call straight away.

When a clogged drain is an emergency

A slow bathroom sink can wait until morning. A drain that fills a tub when you flush a toilet, or a floor drain that bubbles when the washing machine runs, cannot. Both mean the blockage is in the shared line, and the next load of laundry will push dirty water into your basement.

Our line is open 24/7, so call at night or on a weekend and the dispatcher will tell you what to stop using. The plumber arrives with a cable machine and, when needed, a camera, and clears the line the same visit whenever the blockage is reachable.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

These steps cost nothing and keep a backup from spreading.

Stop the water

No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher. Every gallon you send down goes to the lowest open drain.

Protect the floor

Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from basement floor drains and take photos of any water for your insurance file.

Skip the chemicals

Liquid drain openers can sit in a standing pipe and burn the plumber who opens it, so leave them on the shelf.

Note the pattern

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures gurgle or back up. That pattern tells the plumber whether the blockage is local or in the main.

Where the clog is tells you how it gets cleared

Callers describe drains in everyday terms, and each description points to a different part of the system. Here is how the plumber reads them.

One slow kitchen sink

Grease and food residue build up in the trap arm and the first few feet of line. A hand auger or cable usually clears it, and a short jet pass can scour what remains.

A tub or shower that holds water

Hair and soap form a mat in the trap or just past it. A small cable with a retrieving head pulls it out rather than pushing it deeper.

Several fixtures slow at once

That points to the branch or main line. A larger cable machine and a camera find the blockage and show whether the pipe itself is damaged.

A floor drain that rises

The drain is the lowest opening, so it shows trouble from the main line first. Treat it as urgent and avoid water use until it is cleared.

Cable, jet or camera: which fits which clog

Plumbers carry three main tools. Picking the right one saves you from paying for a repeat visit.

ToolBest forLimits
Hand auger or drum machineSink and tub clogs close to the fixtureShort reach, may not clear a full-width coating
Cable machine with cutter headsRoots, mineral scale and solid blockages in branch and main linesOpens a channel but can leave grease on the pipe wall
Hydro jetGrease, soap scum and fine sediment across the whole pipe wallNot suited to a collapsed or badly cracked pipe
Drain cameraFinding where and why a line keeps cloggingA diagnostic tool, not a cleaner

If the same drain clogs twice in a year, ask for a camera look. A clog that returns is rarely bad luck, and the camera shows whether the cause is roots, a belly or a joint that has shifted. Our hydro jetting page explains when a jet is worth the extra cost.

What Haverford homes do to a drain

Haverford Township grew in stages, and the neighborhoods of Havertown, Brookline, Manoa and Oakmont began as separate developments. Older homes in a township like this commonly have cast iron or clay drain lines that roughen with age, and rough pipe catches grease and roots more readily than new plastic does.

Tell the dispatcher how old the house is and which street you are on. A plumber who expects old pipe brings a camera and a longer cable, which cuts the chance of a second trip.

There is a legal side too. Under the Haverford Township Code, a sewer service lateral is the property of the customer, and the Township says it is generally not liable for sewer or drain damage. A clog on your side of the connection is yours to fix, so it pays to clear it before it becomes a backup.

What the visit looks like

A normal drain cleaning visit follows the same order whether it is 2 p.m. or 2 a.m.

  1. Find the cleanout and the symptomThe plumber checks fixtures, locates the nearest cleanout and runs water to see how the line behaves.
  2. Clear the blockageA cable or jet opens the line. The plumber keeps going until water runs freely, not just until it first drains.
  3. Look inside if the clog keeps coming backA camera pass shows roots, breaks or buildup, and you see the footage before any bigger repair is proposed.
  4. Test and explainWater is run through every fixture on the line, and you hear which habits or pipe conditions caused the clog.

Kitchen sink clogs and what actually causes them

Kitchen drains clog from fats that cool and stick, coffee grounds that pack into solid layers and starchy food such as rice or pasta that swells. A rinse with hot water does not keep fat moving once it reaches the cooler pipe under the floor.

A good habit is to wipe pans with a paper towel before washing and to keep a strainer in the sink. If the kitchen drain slows again within weeks of a cleaning, ask the plumber to jet the line. For disposal-related slowdowns, see garbage disposal repair.

Why code cares about drain access

The International Plumbing Code, or IPC, expects cleanouts at certain points in a drain system so the line can be cleared without damage. Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code draws on those model codes.

If your house has no accessible cleanout, adding one is a small job that makes every future cleaning cheaper.

When drain cleaning is not enough

Some blockages are symptoms. A line that clogs every spring may have tree roots entering at a joint. A line that clogs after every storm may be taking in groundwater through a crack. In those cases cleaning is a pause, not a fix.
