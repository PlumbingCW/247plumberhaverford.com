Emergency garbage disposal repair in Haverford

A garbage disposal that hums but does not spin, leaks under the sink or has backed up into the dishwasher needs a quick fix. In Haverford, PA the plumber clears jams, resets motors, replaces seals and installs a new unit when the old one is done.

This page covers the usual failures, what you can safely try first and when to call.

When a disposal problem is urgent

A leak from the bottom of the unit, water backing up into a double sink or the dishwasher or a smell of burning from the motor are urgent. The first can ruin a cabinet. The second means the drain line is blocked.

Turn the disposal off at the switch, and turn off the breaker if it hums and will not stop. Never put your hand into the disposal. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

What you can try before calling

Safe, quick checks that fix many jams.

  1. Switch off and unplugTurn off the switch and, if the cord is reachable, unplug it, or turn off the breaker.
  2. Use the hex keyMost units have a socket on the bottom for a hex wrench. Work it back and forth to free a jam.
  3. Press the resetA red button on the bottom resets an overloaded motor. Wait a few minutes first.
  4. Look with a lightUse a flashlight and tongs, never your fingers, to remove anything visible.

What should and should not go down

Disposals are built for soft food scraps. They struggle with grease, fibrous vegetables like celery and corn husks, coffee grounds in large amounts, pasta and rice, bones and eggshells in bulk.

Grease does the most harm, because it cools in the drain and coats the pipe. If your kitchen line backs up often, the disposal is only part of the story. See drain cleaning for the line and hydro jetting for a cleaner pipe wall.

Repair or replace a disposal

A disposal lasts a handful of years, depending on use. A jam or a seal replacement is worth repairing on a newer unit. A burned-out motor or cracked housing on an older one is better replaced.

Replacement is straightforward on a standard mount. The plumber disconnects the drain, installs the new unit, tests it and checks the dishwasher connection. A larger-horsepower model can handle more but costs more.

Electrical and plumbing notes for a disposal swap

A disposal needs a switched outlet or a hardwired connection that meets the National Electrical Code, or NEC, and a drain connection that meets plumbing code. A plumber handles the drain side and checks the power.

If the unit is hardwired and the wiring looks damaged, an electrician should look at it first.

When the problem is the sink drain

A disposal that spins fine while the sink drains slowly is telling you the line below is the problem. The kitchen drain carries grease and food scraps, and over years it narrows.

A cable or jet clears the line, and a camera shows whether the pipe is damaged. Remember that a repeated kitchen clog may also hint at a larger line problem, discussed on sewer line cleaning.
