Emergency toilet repair in Haverford

A toilet that overflows, will not flush or leaks at the base is a problem that cannot wait, because it is often your only bathroom. In Haverford, PA the plumber clears clogs, replaces worn parts and, when needed, resets or replaces the toilet itself.

This page covers clogs, running toilets, base leaks and the emergency steps for an overflowing bowl.

When a toilet is an emergency

A bowl rising toward the rim, water on the bathroom floor or a toilet that backs up when you run another fixture all call for a quick response. A running toilet that wastes water all day is not a midnight emergency but it does run up your bill.

If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the flow, or close the shutoff valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise. Then call the 24/7 line.

Stop an overflow in four steps

Keep these steps in mind before the plumber arrives.

  1. Close the shutoffThe valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet stops water flowing to the tank. Turn clockwise.
  2. Lift the tank lidPush the flapper down to stop water entering the bowl.
  3. Do not flush againA second flush adds water to a full bowl and spreads the mess.
  4. Mop and ventilateSoak up water quickly and keep children and pets away from contaminated water.

Common toilet problems and what causes them

Most calls fall into a few patterns.

Clog that will not clear

A plunger fails when the blockage is beyond the trap. A closet auger reaches it without damaging the bowl.

Constant running

A worn flapper, a bad fill valve or a chain that is too tight lets water slip into the bowl.

Leak at the base

A failed wax ring or loose closet bolts let water seep out around the floor flange.

Weak flush

Clogged rim jets, a low tank level or a partial blockage in the drain.

When the clog is not the toilet

A toilet that clogs again and again, or that gurgles when another fixture drains, is telling you the clog is in the main line. Plunging will not fix a blocked sewer.

In that case the answer is a drain cable or jet, covered in drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning. Do not use liquid drain openers in a toilet, since they can damage the bowl and splash back on whoever opens the line next.

Water use and your bill

A running toilet can waste a surprising amount of water without any sound. A simple test is to drop a few drops of food coloring into the tank and wait ten minutes. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

Replacing a flapper or fill valve is among the most cost-effective plumbing repairs you can make. If your bill has jumped and you cannot find the cause, a leak detection visit can check the rest of the house.

What code expects of a toilet installation

The International Plumbing Code, or IPC, and federal water efficiency rules limit how much water a toilet may use per flush. Replacing a toilet with a current model meets those limits, while an old unit may use more than the rules now allow.

If your toilet is decades old and keeps failing, replacing it can lower your water use as well as fix the leak.

When to replace instead

A toilet with a cracked bowl, a failing glaze or a chronic poor flush is usually better replaced than repaired. A newer model also uses less water per flush.

Our plumber can install a standard replacement as part of the same visit. For a bathroom remodel with a toilet move, see plumbing installation.
