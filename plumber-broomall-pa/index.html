Broomall neighborhoods and what each needs

Broomall is a large suburb in Marple Township, west of Haverford Township and reached by PA 3 and I-476. It is a separate community with its own streets, shopping and identity, and most homes there sit on residential streets built in the postwar decades.

Postwar houses often have copper supply lines and cast iron drains, and many have basements. That points to sump pumps, water heaters and old drain lines as the common calls.

Marple Township and where permits go

Broomall is in Marple Township, which is a different municipality from Haverford Township. That means permits, inspections and sewer rules come from Marple Township's offices, not from Haverford's Building and Codes Department.

Ask the plumber to confirm which office applies before work that needs a permit. Springfield is a nearby community, and we serve it too from the same dispatch.

Basements and floor drains in postwar Broomall homes

Many Broomall homes have basements with a floor drain, a sump pit or both. The floor drain connects to the same lateral that serves the rest of the house, so a drain that bubbles during heavy rain is often a sign of a sewer line under load.

Check that your floor drain has a working trap and, if you have a sump, that the pump is running on its own. A plumber can add a backwater valve to protect the floor drain, which is covered on our plumbing installation page.

Seasonal checks for a Broomall house

In late winter, disconnect hoses and look at pipes in the garage and exterior walls. In spring, test the sump pump and clear the gutters. In fall, check the water heater and the heating system before the cold arrives.

These checks take an hour and prevent most of the emergency calls we see from this side of the Blue Route.
