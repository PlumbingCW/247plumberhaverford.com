Faucet repair in Haverford, PA

A dripping faucet, a leaking handle or a stuck valve wastes water and can damage a cabinet if it goes unnoticed. In Haverford, PA the plumber replaces cartridges, seats, O-rings and supply lines, and replaces the whole faucet when it is worn out.

This page covers kitchen, bathroom, laundry and outdoor faucets, as well as the emergency case of a faucet that will not shut off.

When a faucet leak is urgent

A faucet that will not shut off, a supply line spraying under the sink or a handle that has broken free is an emergency. Water under a cabinet can ruin the floor and mold the wood within days.

Close the shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If they do not turn, close the main water valve and call the 24/7 line. A slow drip can wait until morning, but the sooner you fix it, the less it costs.

What the symptom usually means

Different drips point to different parts.

Drip from the spout

A worn washer, seat or cartridge inside the valve. Repair is quick and inexpensive.

Leak at the base of the handle

A worn O-ring or loose packing nut.

Low flow

A clogged aerator, a partly closed shutoff or mineral buildup in the cartridge.

Water under the sink

A supply line, shutoff valve or drain connection may be leaking, not the faucet itself.

Mineral buildup and aerators

Mineral deposits build up inside faucets and clog aerators, which causes low flow or a spray that goes sideways. Unscrewing the aerator and soaking it in vinegar often restores flow.

If you see white or green crust on the spout, your water carries minerals that will return. The plumber can test your water and tell you if a filter or softener might help. We do not quote a hardness number for the neighborhood, because it varies by street and source.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

The small valves under a sink are meant to be turned only a few times in their life, and old ones often seize or leak when touched. Braided supply lines can also fail after years of use.

When a plumber replaces your faucet, new supply lines and, if needed, new shutoffs are part of the work. A faucet job with an old valve is a good time to fix it, because a valve that will not close is a flood waiting to happen.

What to do when a faucet will not shut off

Close the shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If they do not turn, close the main water valve and open the lowest tap to drain the line. Do not force a stuck valve, since a broken one can turn a drip into a flood.

Place a bucket under the leak, dry the cabinet floor and move anything stored there. Then call, so the plumber arrives knowing the shutoffs may need replacing.

Faucet standards you can check

Faucets are tested to plumbing standards such as ASME A112.18.1, which covers performance and materials, and low-lead requirements apply to fixtures that supply drinking water. Look for the markings on the box or the fixture.

Buying a faucet with those markings is a simple way to avoid poor-quality parts that fail early.

Outdoor faucets and winter

Outdoor hose bibs can freeze and split inside the wall if a hose is left attached in winter. The leak may not show until spring, when water runs inside the wall.

Disconnect hoses before the first freeze and shut off the interior valve if you have one. If you see water behind a wall near a hose bib, call and read about burst pipe repair. For a kitchen disposal near the sink, see garbage disposal repair.
