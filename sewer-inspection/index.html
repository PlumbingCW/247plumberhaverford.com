What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera on a flexible rod through your drain line and records what the pipe looks like inside. In Haverford, PA it is how a plumber confirms roots, cracks, bellies and breaks before anyone suggests a repair.

This is a planned service as often as an emergency one. Homeowners schedule it before buying a house, after a clog that keeps returning or when they want proof before paying for a repair.

When to schedule a camera inspection

The best times to look are before a purchase, after the second clog in a year, when a smell or soft ground appears outside and when a repair quote seems expensive. A camera turns a guess into evidence.

If sewage is already backing up, call the 24/7 line instead. The plumber clears the stoppage first and runs the camera as part of the visit, and the inspection becomes the first step of a repair.

How the inspection runs

A good inspection is more than a short video.

  1. Locate accessThe plumber finds a cleanout or pulls a toilet so the camera can enter the lateral.
  2. Run and recordThe camera travels toward the street while the plumber watches the screen and records footage.
  3. Mark the problemA locator on the camera head shows depth and position above ground, so any damage can be marked in the yard.
  4. Review with youYou see the footage and hear what is minor, what is serious and what can wait.

Haverford homes and the lateral question

Haverford Township's code treats the sewer service lateral as the customer's property. That means a buyer who inherits a cracked lateral inherits the repair bill, and the Township notes that sewer backup is excluded from standard homeowners policies unless an endorsement is added.

For that reason a camera look before closing is worth considering for any older house in Havertown, Brookline, Oakmont or Manoa. Ask the seller's agent whether a recent inspection exists, and ask your insurance agent about sewer backup coverage at the same time.

What an inspection does not tell you

A camera sees the inside of the pipe it can reach. It cannot tell you the soil outside, and it cannot go past a collapsed section. Some rods cannot turn sharp corners or enter through small cleanouts, so reach has limits.

A good report says how far the camera went and why it stopped. If it stopped early, ask what that means and what the next option is, such as access from another point.

Next steps once you have footage

If the footage is clean, you leave with evidence and a note of the pipe's age in hand. If it shows roots, schedule sewer line cleaning or hydro jetting. If it shows damage, the sewer line repair page explains the options.

The same camera can check branch drains too, such as a kitchen line that runs under a slab or a basement floor drain. Mention any drain that has given you trouble, and the plumber can include it.
